# Shot list — what is on screen, and when

Times are from the first frame of `out/ulm-1500.mp4`. Total running time
3 min 32 s.

| Time | Shot | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | High approach from the south-east | The title over the Danube valley. This is the viewpoint of the 1493 Schedel woodcut — the oldest surviving picture of Ulm — which shows the city as a panorama from the south. |
| 0:10–0:25 | Descent towards the river | The walled town on the north bank; strip fields on both sides; the Alb rising behind. |
| 0:25–0:38 | The bridge | The Herdbrucke: timber deck on stone piers, the only crossing. The toll chapel at its far end. Barges and a timber raft on the water. |
| 0:38–0:58 | Along the river wall, heading west | The wall standing **in** the Danube (1480). The leaning **Metzgerturm** (c. 1340), then the round **Adlerbastei** (c. 1480). Boats moored under the brick. |
| 0:58–1:20 | Banking over the Blau | The fishermen's and tanners' quarter: two arms of the stream, mills with undershot wheels, the tightest, lowest housing in the city. |
| 1:20–1:35 | The Minster comes up | The church appears as it was: a huge body of masonry with a **truncated** west tower. |
| 1:30–1:56 | Orbit of the Minster | The square shaft ends at about 70 m in a **platform** — the one Maximilian I climbed in 1492. Above it the five-metre stump of the octagon under a **pyramidal emergency roof**, the working stage still standing round it, and a treadwheel crane on the platform. The choir towers are stumps too. Note that houses crowd right up to the church: the open Münsterplatz of today is a 19th-century clearance. |
| 1:56–2:12 | Down to the market | The **Rathaus** — a plain gabled block with an arcaded ground floor. No painted façade, no astronomical clock; both arrive after 1520. Market stalls under cloth awnings. |
| 2:12–2:35 | Out over the north-west wall | Gate towers with the city's black-and-white banner; the ditch and low outer wall; convent precincts inside. |
| 2:35–2:55 | The city's own countryside | Strip fields in blocks of differing direction — the three-field rotation. Roads running to each gate. The gallows on its hill. |
| 2:55–3:10 | The bleaching greens | White strips on the meadows by the water: **fustian (Barchent)** laid out to bleach, some 60,000 pieces a year. This is what paid for the Minster. |
| 3:10–3:32 | The climb away, and the end card | The whole circuit in one frame: about 3.5 km of brick wall, some 85 hectares, the Danube, the Blau, and the unfinished tower still the tallest thing for a hundred kilometres. |

## Three things to notice because they are *not* there

1. **No spire.** Everyone's mental image of Ulm is the 161.5 m tower of 1890.
2. **No Neue Straße.** The straight road through the old town was driven
   through the ruins after 1944.
3. **Nothing on the far bank.** Neu-Ulm is founded in 1810. In 1500 that side
   is meadow, field and the road to Bavaria.
